## Control statement

Resolution Closure Atlas F0.1 records recurring higher-order groupings found in the
technical corpus and in external-model readings of that corpus. It asks what
the grouping preserves, what it suppresses, when it must reopen, and whether
the grouping is equivalence, composition, morphology, or an ordered mixture.

This pack does not:

- add an organ, office, authority, runtime gate, or automatic classifier;
- amend the V1 Architectural Closure Record or any embodiment freeze;
- claim that repeated language proves a latent architecture;
- treat approximate similarity as equality;
- authorize use of a coarse view for consequence;
- establish a new theorem or a general closure calculus.

It is a successor clarification of the existing multiresolution and
non-collapse work. Its job is to keep an observed macro-pattern available for
attack without erasing the differentiated carriers that produced it.

Keeper:

> One at one resolution. Many at another. Never fewer than consequence permits.

## 1. Terminology boundary

The corpus already uses **closure** in several legitimate senses. They must not
be silently merged.

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Architectural closure | A ratified membership or topology freeze |
| Proof or receipt closure | Completion of a proof/return chain |
| Obligation closure | Explicit settlement of a required obligation |
| Resolution closure | A coarse view, functional assembly, or recurring typed pattern recorded by this Atlas |

`Resolution closure` is project shorthand. It is not asserted to be a
mathematical closure operator; no monotonicity, extensivity, or idempotence
claim is made.

## 3. Three different objects

The three primary closure kinds are deliberately not unified into one
mathematical operator.

In `atlas.json`, `closure_type` records the proposed or rejected typing and
`typing_status` records whether that typing is established, candidate, or
rejected. `EQUIVALENCE` with `typing_status: CANDIDATE` does not assert that an
equivalence relation has already been supplied.

### 3.1 Equivalence abstraction

An equivalence abstraction is a map

\[
\alpha:X_K\to M
\]

from admissible fine states or histories to a coarse view. Two fine objects
share a macro-object exactly when they lie in the same fiber of \(\alpha\).
This kind requires an actual equivalence relation and, for future-sensitive
use, congruence under the admitted continuation family.

### 3.2 Compositional assembly

A compositional assembly contracts a typed open subsystem \(S\) to a
boundary-visible macro-component \(m_S\):

\[
(S,\partial S,\mathcal A_S,\mathcal G_S)\rightsquigarrow m_S.
\]

Here \(\partial S\) is the declared interface, \(\mathcal A_S\) the environment
assumptions, and \(\mathcal G_S\) the guarantees. Members remain distinct.
Membership alone is insufficient: wiring, order, hidden channels, shared
resources, and assumptions can change the macro-behavior.

### 3.3 Morphological classification

A morphological classification assigns a typed occurrence to a declared
pattern:

\[
\mu:G\to P.
\]

It says that selected roles and relations instantiate a common form. It does
not imply behavioral equivalence, substitutability, authority transfer, or
safe suppression.

### 3.4 Mixed trace

An observed grouping may involve several operations. The Atlas records a typed
ordered log

\[
Z_0\xrightarrow{o_1}Z_1\xrightarrow{o_2}\cdots
\xrightarrow{o_n}Z_n
\]

with every input, output, and \(o_i\) typed as equivalence, composition, or
morphology. Composition notation is justified only when adjacent output and
input types agree. The Atlas does not store `MIXED` as an unexplained bucket
because the operations need not commute.

## 4. Equivalence-view adequacy

Let:

- \(X_K\) be the independently specified admissible concrete states or
  histories under profile \(K\);
- \(\alpha:X_K\to M\) be a resolution map, with
  \(M=\operatorname{im}(\alpha)\) (equivalently, take \(\alpha\) to be
  surjective onto the declared view space);
- \(F_{K,a}:X_K\to Y_a\) be the independently specified consequential
  semantics for exact action \(a\).

The output \(Y_a\) may include admissibility, exact effect, external
observation, receipt, successor standing, cost, probability, or bounded future
continuations when those are material.

The view is adequate for \((K,a)\) exactly when the consequential semantics
factor through it:

\[
\operatorname{Adequate}_{K,a}(\alpha)
\Longleftrightarrow
\exists\bar F_{K,a}\;F_{K,a}=\bar F_{K,a}\circ\alpha.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\ker(\alpha)\subseteq\ker(F_{K,a}).
\]

Define the bad fiber pairs:

\[
\operatorname{Bad}(\alpha,K,a)=
\{(x,x')\mid \alpha(x)=\alpha(x')\land
F_{K,a}(x)\ne F_{K,a}(x')\}.
\]

Then a coarse view may decide \(a\) only if
\(\operatorname{Bad}(\alpha,K,a)=\varnothing\). For an action family \(A\), the
required partition is bounded by

\[
\ker(\alpha)\subseteq\bigcap_{a\in A}\ker(F_{K,a}).
\]

Adding actions or continuations can only require the same or a finer
resolution. This is a standard task-relative view-determinacy condition, not
a novelty claim.

This equation applies directly to a total deterministic consequential
semantics. A partial semantics must first expose `UNDEFINED` or `HOLD` as an
explicit outcome. A nondeterministic or probabilistic semantics must compare
the declared outcome sets, traces, or distributions under an independently
specified observation relation. Compositional assemblies instead need a
boundary trace/refinement result under explicit environment assumptions.
Morphological classification normally makes no safe-substitution claim at
all.

### Runtime disposition

If adequacy is absent, invalidated, or refuted:

1. refine or reopen the relevant fiber;
2. bind the exact action, canonical final arguments, profile, source head, and
   witness version;
3. re-evaluate atomically before the first irreversible effect;
4. `HOLD` if the required resolution cannot be lawfully obtained.

If the failure is discovered after an external sink may have accepted the
effect, `HOLD` is no longer an accurate disposition. The state is
`UNSETTLED`, `AMBIGUOUS`, or `BREACH`, according to the bounded recovery
semantics.

## 5. Witness is not certificate

\[
\boxed{\text{ClosureWitness}\ne\text{AdequacyCertificate}}
\]

A closure witness supports attribution or reopening. It may contain one or
more independently typed parts:

- reconstructive complement;
- immutable or version-bound source handle;
- derivation provenance;
- integrity binding over members, relations, profile, purpose, and state head;
- reopen policy and active invalidation metadata.

An adequacy certificate proves that one declared resolution is sufficient for
one declared action family under one declared profile and semantics. Retaining
the complete fine object as a witness makes reopening easy, but by itself
proves neither adequacy nor nontrivial storage compression.

## 6. Resolution and participation geometry

Raw resolution maps form a preorder under factorization: one view is no finer
than another when it factors through the other. Mutual factorization need not
make the maps literally equal. Their kernels—or views modulo mutual
determinacy/isomorphism—form the corresponding partial order. This geometry is
not generally a single ladder: for a fine state \(x=(p,q)\), the view retaining
only \(p\) and the view retaining only \(q\) are incomparable.

Let \(P=\operatorname{Ports}(V)\), let \(\pi:P\to V\) map each role port to its
carrier, let \(\tau_P:P\to T_P\) type the ports, and let
\(\tau_C:I\to T_C\) type the macro-functions. Functional participation is an
overlapping typed hypergraph:

\[
\mathcal H=(V,P,\pi,\tau_P,\{(C_i,\tau_C(i))\}_{i\in I}),
\qquad C_i\subseteq P.
\]

The Atlas records role slices or ports rather than claiming that a whole
object is absorbed into one macro-function. Overlap must not duplicate
authority, quota, evidence, or standing.

Each record calls the inspected differences `resolution_distinctions`. Some
are actually suppressed and witnessed; others are explicitly prohibited from
suppression. The field name therefore does not presuppose that forgetting was
lawful.

For local views \(m_1,\ldots,m_n\), define their joint concretization:

\[
\Gamma(m_1,\ldots,m_n)=
\{x\in X_K\mid \alpha_i(x)=m_i\text{ for all }i\}.
\]

- \(\Gamma=\varnothing\): the local views are incompatible;
- \(\Gamma\ne\varnothing\) but \(F_{K,a}\) varies on \(\Gamma\): resolution is
  insufficient;
- \(F_{K,a}\) is constant on this \(\Gamma\): the observed joint tuple is
  sufficient for the declared action.

Global adequacy of the joint-view map requires the same constancy on every
nonempty joint fiber, not merely on the observed tuple.

This does not require all local views to fuse into one globally uniform
account. Typed dissent and explicit remainder may remain.

### Coherence, cohesion, and adequacy

The recurring closures expose three different burdens:

\[
\text{coherence}\ne\text{cohesion}\ne\text{adequacy}.
\]

- **Coherence** asks whether differentiated parts or local views are rightly
  related for the present profile.
- **Cohesion** asks whether that differentiated relation persists as one
  attributable functional whole across time, replacement, and resolution
  change.
- **Adequacy** asks whether the chosen view or boundary contract is sufficient
  for one declared consequential use.

Cohesion explains why an observed macro-object can remain recognizable without
its members becoming identical. It still supplies no action permission: a
cohesive assembly can be inadequate for a particular decision and must then
reopen or hold.

## 7. What the first Atlas shows

The machine-readable Atlas records thirteen actual or proposed closure events.
Four conclusions survive the pass.

### 7.1 The external closures are not one phenomenon

The recurring groupings separate into:

- components jointly realizing a boundary-level function;
- different systems exhibiting the same transformation morphology;
- a candidate same-view relation that still needs action-relative adequacy;
- several false collapses where role, authority, lineage, or exact action was
  suppressed.

Treating all of them as quotient classes would erase the signal the external
model exposed.

### 7.2 The architecture is a cover, not an organ tree

Counterpart navigation may participate in translation and orientation. A
receipt mechanism may participate in proof, return, and continuity. These are
overlapping role slices, not multiple copies of authority and not evidence
that the participating object is identical to each macro-function.

### 7.3 The proposed five-term cycle is not yet five organs

The observed sequence

\[
\text{FIELD}\to\text{REFRACT}\to\text{TRANSLATE}\to
\text{GOVERN}\to\text{RETURN}
\]

remains a useful morphological candidate. It does not yet emerge as five peer
types: `FIELD` names a state/configuration space while the other terms mostly
name transformations or burdens. The stronger current finding is the general
resolution grammar:

\[
\text{differentiate}\to\text{form a bounded view or assembly}
\to\text{test consequence}\to\text{reopen if required}\to\text{return}.
\]

The Atlas therefore preserves the five-term recurrence without installing it
as anatomy.

### 7.4 Why the external model closes

The closure event itself is useful data. The present pass identifies four
competing causes rather than dismissing the event as mere model error:

1. **Functional altitude.** Distinct carriers answer one boundary-level
   question and therefore become one macro-object when viewed from farther
   away.
2. **Morphological recurrence.** Several mechanisms share a stable typed
   transformation pattern even though their semantics and authority differ.
3. **Prompt-induced alignment.** A request for one-to-one translation or a
   supplied naming table encourages the model to complete a correspondence
   grid.
4. **Compression pressure.** Summary generation rewards fewer nouns and can
   erase distinctions whose importance is not visible in the requested output.

The first two can reveal natural resolution boundaries. The latter two can
manufacture apparent architecture. Distinguishing them requires retaining the
exact prompt, model/version, output, and run conditions, then repeating the
coding under label permutation, blind definitions, explicit anti-collapse
instructions, unrelated negative controls, and multiple models. Stable closure
under those attacks is evidence for a macro-pattern; it is still not proof of
equivalence, composition, implementation, or authority.

### 7.5 The Becoming Spine is frozen as a conformance view

The published-estate audit found that the proposed temporal spine is not a
missing ontology waiting to be invented. Its carriers are already distributed
across prospective readiness, SourcePoint authorship, CM-Core/ONE-RCC, TOP,
RGCB, Golden Crossing, CHR, PBR, and the ONE V1 bounded assemblies.

The linked F0.1 reconciliation therefore freezes this human-facing view:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Possibility}\to\text{Preparation}\to\text{Choice}
\to\text{Commitment}\to\text{Attempt}\to\text{Occurrence}
\to\text{Consequence}\\
&\to\text{Witness}\to\text{Evidence}\to\text{Return}
\to\text{Reconstruction}\to\text{Settlement}\to\text{Inheritance}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The native topology remains a typed causal hypergraph with joins, branches,
retries, open states, and conditional successor admission. The thirteen terms
are conformance burdens, not a claim of thirteen peer runtime records.

The conservation pair is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Standing may not increase without an explicit typed transition;}
\quad
\text{consequential differentiation may not decrease without adequacy.}
}
\]

The component non-promotion and recoverable-compression laws are existing. The
pairing and action-relative form are a new derivation. See
`CONSTITUTIONAL-BECOMING-SPINE-F0.1.md` and
`becoming-spine-crosswalk.json` for the complete genealogy and carrier map.

The reconciliation also exposes two actual defects rather than using them to
downgrade the whole estate:

1. ONE-RCC and TOP use `AttemptAdmission` with incompatible attempt
   cardinality; the profiles need qualified types or an explicit adapter.
2. The MUS settlement harness reports 17/17 without schema-validating the mock
   receipt it emits; that payload currently lacks required nested fields.

## 8. Bounded strict-middle specimen

The smallest promising finite specimen is already latent in the BodyUnity
fixtures:

1. `BUF-007` supplies two fine states that differ in a protected private field
   while emitting the same authorized bounded return and posture.
2. `BUF-002` supplies a second pair where a material conflict changes the
   lawful downstream disposition and therefore cannot be merged.
3. `EA-001` supplies the negative point-of-effect witness: a view bound to the
   approved tool/arguments is not adequate for a transformed final dispatch.

Let \(E:X_K\to S\) be an independently specified endpoint map. With \(K\),
\(E\), \(F_{K,a}\), and the continuation horizon specified independently, the
first two pairs could demonstrate

\[
\Delta_X\subsetneq\ker(\alpha)\subsetneq\ker(E),
\]

for a bounded finite model: some raw differences are safely hidden, while
some same-endpoint differences must remain visible. The present fixture
evaluator checks a declared list of material distinctions; it does not prove
that the list is complete. In particular, `BUF-002` supplies the
must-distinguish burden but does not yet establish that its selected pair lies
in \(\ker(E)\); that same-endpoint witness must be constructed and proved. The
strict middle is therefore a proof target, not an established theorem.

## 9. Chiasm research priority

The SourcePoint has identified chiasm as a foundational research object: a
crossing-and-return structure that may make loss or corruption legible through
broken correspondence. The Atlas preserves that priority as a morphological
candidate rather than reducing it to a decorative metaphor.

The strongest current attack is equally concrete. Bilateral or mirrored form
is not automatically a cryptographic hash or error-correcting code: some
substitutions, paired deletions, or adversarially symmetric changes can retain
the visible pattern. A computational chiasm therefore needs an independently
specified encoding, correspondence relation, protected invariants, threat
model, and detection/collision bound. If those are supplied, the research
question becomes testable:

> Which classes of transformation faults are detected by a typed
> crossing-and-return correspondence, and what redundancy is minimally
> required?

That burden remains open. The pattern is retained; the security claim is not
silently granted.

The hostile fixture pack includes:

- joint dependence missed by field-local sensitivity;
- same members with different wiring/order;
- profile drift invalidating a prior certificate;
- final-action mutation after approval;
- witness present but not consulted;
- overlap amplifying authority or quota;
- a positive control where one carrier participates twice without duplicating
  its grant or quota;
- incompatible local views with no global realization;
- noncommuting mixed closures;
- morphology laundering a semantic inversion;
- privacy or deletion preventing lawful reopening;
- late failure after possible external acceptance;
- witness trivialization by retaining the complete source;
- a shared `AttemptAdmission` label hiding incompatible cardinality;
- preparation silently promoted into SourcePoint intent;
- a correct endpoint redeeming a breached path;
- later reconstruction rewriting earlier knowledge;
- persistence silently promoted into inheritance;
- a generated settlement receipt escaping its own schema validator.

The expected dispositions distinguish `REJECT_CLOSURE`, `REFINE_OR_REOPEN`,
`HOLD`, and post-effect `UNSETTLED_OR_AMBIGUOUS`. A pattern-only match receives
`MORPHOLOGICAL_CANDIDATE_ONLY`.

## 11. Known-work collision

The formal core overlaps established work:

- quotients, contextual equivalence, Myhill–Nerode relations, bisimulation,
  and lumpability for equivalence abstraction;
- abstract interpretation, strong preservation, and CEGAR for safe reduced
  views and refinement;
- database view determinacy and query rewriting for the factorization test;
- lenses and complements for reconstruction from view plus retained residue;
- provenance systems for derivation history;
- hypergraphs/covers for overlapping participation;
- open systems, wiring diagrams, and assume-guarantee contracts for
  compositional assemblies;
- typed graph patterns and graph grammars for morphology.

No novelty is claimed from renaming these families. The strongest possible
future novelty ceiling would require a theorem that combines typed overlapping
open systems, history-sensitive actions, provenance-bound reopening, and
fail-closed point-of-effect refinement with compositional soundness,
minimality, decidability, and complexity bounds. None of those results is
proved here.
